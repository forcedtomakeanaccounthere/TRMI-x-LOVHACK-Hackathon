# LinkRoom demo video: script

Length: about 2:30, 1920×1080, 30 fps. Narration is in `script.json`; edit it there and re-record (see README).

### The internet is single-player  
`0:00.00` · 10.6s

**Voiceover:** The internet is single-player. We share a link, everyone reads it alone, and the conversation happens somewhere else. LinkRoom puts the people back on the page.

**On screen:** Big serif line "The internet is *single-player.*" types in word by word. The browser window rises out of the page in 2.5D onto the landing page's "The problem" section (slow push-in), then cuts to the hero "Every link, read together." and pulls back.

### Your reading HQ  
`0:10.60` · 11.7s

**Voiceover:** Every link and every PDF becomes a live room. Your dashboard shows which rooms are running, who is reading right now, your study groups, and a shared calendar, all in one place.

**On screen:** Dashboard overview. Camera pushes into the dark "Running · 4" card (callout: 4 rooms running · 9 reading now), then the Groups & projects board, then the shared November calendar.

### Multiple documents, one room  
`0:22.33` · 15.8s

**Voiceover:** Add as many sources as you need. Drop in PDFs, Word, PowerPoint and Markdown files, paste a web link, or pull straight from Google Drive. Every source sits side by side, and you choose which ones the AI should use.

**On screen:** Add-sources dialog: zoom on the drop zone (PDF · Word · PowerPoint · Markdown), the Link tab with an arXiv URL, the Google Drive button, then a real room with 9 sources where the left column is spotlit (choose what the AI reads).

### YouTube to document  
`0:38.13` · 11.6s

**Voiceover:** Got a lecture on YouTube? Paste the link, and LinkRoom reads the captions, turning the video into a timestamped document your group can highlight and question, just like text.

**On screen:** YouTube tab with a link being typed (keyboard SFX), then the processed lecture: a "Video transcript" document with timestamps 0:00, 0:42, 1:31… The camera scrolls down the transcript.

### Annotations and text boxes  
`0:49.73` · 12.8s

**On screen:** Same framing on The Federalist No. 10 while marks appear one by one: yellow highlight, underline, a text box ("Key definition — memorise for Friday!"), then pull back to the full annotated page and the tool dock.
